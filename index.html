Sambahsa for English Speakers

This learning course is inspired by Margarita Madrigal and Language Transfer.

With very little grammatical explanation, you will begin to internalize the language. Don't try to memorize anything! Just take your time and really think through how to translate the phrases. And mistakes are ok! Each lesson will build upon what you learned before it. Think of this course like a puzzle. You are building neural pathways :)

Lesson 1

The verb stem for want is eiskw-.

To say "I want", you just add an "o" on the end.

I want.

Eiskwo. (pronounced like "ace-kwo")

To say "you want", you just add an "s" on the end.

You want.

Eiskws. (pronounced like "ace-kus")

The verb stem for love is lieubh-.

Just like before, to say "I love", you add an "o" on the end.

I love.

Lieubho. (pronounced like "lyoob-ho")

You love.

Lieubhs. (pronounced like "lyoobs")

To negate a verb, you place ne (pronounced like "neh") before it.

I don't want.

Ne eiskwo.

You don't love.

Ne lieubhs.

The word for him is iom (pronounced like "yom")

I love him.

Lieubho iom.

You don't want him.

Ne eiskws iom.

In this lesson, you have learned:

  • eiskw-
  • lieubh-
  • -o
  • -s
  • ne
  • iom

Lesson 2

When you want to say he, she, or it does something, you add a -t to the verb stem.

He/She/It wants.

Eiskwt.

He/She/It doesn't love.

Ne lieubht.

To specify if the doer is a man, woman, or it, you can use the pronoun before.

The word for he is is (pronounced like "ease" but with a true s, not a z sound.)

He loves him.

Is lieubht iom.

The word for me is me (pronounced like "meh")

He wants me.

Is eiskwt me.

You love me.

Lieubhs me.

In this lesson, you have learned:

  • -t
  • is
  • iam
  • me

Lesson 3

The word for you is te (pronounced like "teh")

I love you.

Lieubho te.

He wants you.

Is eiskwt te.

You don't love him.

Ne lieubhs iom.

The word for it is id (pronounced like "eat" with a d instead of t)

I want it.

Eiskwo id.

The word for dog is kwaun (pronounced like "wow" with a k on the front and n on the end.)

In Sambahsa, you don't use the word "the". Instead, depending on what the gender is (he, she, it), you use those pronouns instead.

So instead of "the (male) cat wants it", you say "he cat wants it".

The (male) dog loves him.

Is kwaun lieubht iom.

He loves the (male) dog.

Is lieubht iom kwaun.

What if you don't know the gender of the animal or person?

The word for he/she/it/him/her/it (unknown gender) is el.

He loves the (unknown gender) dog.

Is lieubht el kwaun.

The (unknown gender) dog wants you.

El kwaun eiskwt te.

In most cases, animals will take the unknown / undetermined gender.

In this lesson, you have learned:

  • te
  • id
  • kwaun
  • el

Lesson 4

The verb stem for give is dah-.

The word for to him is ei (pronounced like "day" without the d).

I give it to him.

Daho id ei.

You give it to him.

Dahs id ei.

The word for to me is just mi (pronounced like "me").

He doesn't give it to me.

Is ne daht id mi.

I give the (unknown gender) dog to him.

Daho el kwaun ei.

The word for to you is tib (pronounced like "teeb").

I give him to you.

Daho iom tib.

The word for but is bet.

A lot of times, an extra pronoun like "it" isn't needed if the context makes it clear.

You give it to him, but he doesn't want it.

Dahs id ei, bet is ne eiskwt.

  • dah-
  • ei
  • mi
  • tib
  • bet
